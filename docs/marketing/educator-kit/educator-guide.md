# epac Educator Guide

Audience: Canadian politics, public policy, civics, journalism, and social studies instructors.

epac is a free iOS app that helps students follow Parliament using official public records: Hansard, recorded votes, bills, MP profiles, expenditures, Elections Canada data, Statistics Canada references, and lobbying registry records. It is useful in courses where students need to connect institutions, representation, and policy outcomes to primary-source evidence.

## Learning Outcomes

Students using this kit should be able to:

- Identify their Member of Parliament and riding using an official source.
- Read Hansard as a primary source, distinguishing a speech, question, answer, and procedural entry.
- Trace a federal bill through its parliamentary stages.
- Compare an MP's recorded votes with party, riding, and issue context.
- Cite official parliamentary and government sources in a short analysis.
- Explain the limits of what government records can and cannot show.

## Suggested Course Uses

### One-class activity

Use epac during a lecture on representation. Ask students to find their MP, one recent vote, and one recent speech. In pairs, students compare whether the vote and speech address the same policy area.

### Two-week module

Assign the sample "Track Your MP for 30 Days" activity. Students collect three pieces of source-linked evidence: a speech, a recorded vote, and either an expense disclosure or bill interaction. The final product is a short memo, not an opinion essay.

### Semester-long portfolio

Students choose a riding or policy topic and maintain a source log. They add one official record each week, classify it, and write a short note on how it changes their understanding of parliamentary work.

## Classroom Workflow

1. Ask students to install epac from the App Store or pair students who do not have access to an iPhone.
2. Start with the FAQ page at `https://epac.riddimsoftware.com/faq.html` to establish terms such as Hansard, riding, Question Period, recorded vote, and private member's bill.
3. Have students identify a riding and MP.
4. Have students capture source links for each claim they make.
5. Grade the quality of the evidence trail, not whether students agree with an MP.

## Source Discipline

epac should be treated as a navigation layer over official records, not as a substitute for citation. Students should cite the underlying institution wherever possible:

- House of Commons Hansard for speeches and debate text.
- House of Commons recorded divisions for votes.
- LEGISinfo for bill stages and bill text.
- Elections Canada for riding and electoral information.
- Statistics Canada for census context.
- Commissioner of Lobbying registry for lobbying activity.

## Privacy and Access Notes

epac does not require an account. In-app followed MPs, bills, topics, and postal-code preferences are stored on the device. For students who cannot use the iOS app, the website provides public FAQ, topic, riding, and blog pages that can support a parallel web-based activity.

## Assessment Rubric

| Criterion | Strong | Developing | Needs work |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Source trail | Every factual claim points to an official record | Most claims are sourced | Claims rely on news, opinion, or unsupported summaries |
| Institutional understanding | Correctly explains MP, party, bill, vote, and riding roles | Minor terminology errors | Confuses institutions or procedures |
| Analysis | Connects evidence to a clear civic question | Describes records without much synthesis | Makes broad claims unsupported by records |
| Neutrality | Uses precise, non-partisan language | Some loaded language | Advocacy replaces analysis |

## Instructor Checklist

- Confirm the assignment fits the course privacy and accessibility policy.
- Provide a non-iOS alternative for students who need it.
- Require source links or screenshots of official records.
- Remind students that epac is independent and is not affiliated with the Government of Canada.
