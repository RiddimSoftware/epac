# epac Data Sources Explainer

epac only displays data that traces to official public sources. It is not a news app, a commentary app, or an AI-generated summary product. This explainer helps instructors and students evaluate what kind of evidence each source can support.

## Source Map

| Source | What it supports in epac | Authority level | Classroom caveat |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| House of Commons Hansard | Debate text, Question Period, speeches, speaker attribution | Official verbatim parliamentary record | Hansard records what was said, not whether a claim was true |
| House of Commons recorded divisions | MP votes, party-line comparisons, vote outcomes | Official voting record | A vote shows a decision, not every reason behind it |
| LEGISinfo | Bill stages, bill text, sponsor, chamber status | Official legislative information system | A bill's status does not measure public support |
| House of Commons proactive disclosure | MP travel, hospitality, and contract expenses | Official quarterly disclosure | Expenditures need role and geography context |
| Elections Canada | Ridings, electoral history, party and candidate data | Independent election administrator | Boundaries and representation can change after redistribution |
| Statistics Canada | Census and riding context references | National statistical agency | Census variables should not be treated as individual-level facts |
| Commissioner of Lobbying registry | Lobbying registrations and communication reports | Official lobbying registry | A communication report shows contact, not agreement or influence |

## How Students Should Cite epac Work

Use epac to find the record, then cite the official source behind it. For example:

- "House of Commons, Debates (Hansard), sitting date..."
- "House of Commons, Recorded Division..."
- "Parliament of Canada, LEGISinfo, Bill C-..."
- "Elections Canada, electoral district profile..."

Screenshots are useful for classroom review, but source URLs and dates are stronger evidence.

## What epac Does Not Claim

epac does not rank MPs by ideology, score parties by preference, infer motive, summarize debates with generative AI, or decide whether a political statement is true. Those are analytical tasks for students, instructors, journalists, researchers, or dedicated fact-checking organizations.

## Suggested Verification Exercise

Ask students to select one epac item and trace it back to the official source:

1. Pick a speech, vote, bill, or expense item.
2. Identify the institution that published it.
3. Locate the original record.
4. Write two sentences explaining what the record proves.
5. Write one sentence explaining what the record does not prove.
