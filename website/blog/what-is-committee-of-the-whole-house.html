What is Committee of the Whole House?

April 27, 2026 · 3 min read

Parliamentary procedure contains several distinct modes the House of Commons can operate in. One of the lesser-known is "Committee of the Whole House" — a mode where all 338 MPs sitting in the chamber effectively transform into a committee, operating under slightly different rules to enable more flexible and faster debate on specific legislation.

When Is It Used?

Committee of the Whole House is used when the government wants to consider a bill's detailed clauses quickly and collectively, rather than sending them to a smaller standing committee. It is commonly used for urgent or politically sensitive bills, supply bills (budget appropriations), and bills affecting privileges of Parliament itself — where all MPs, not just committee members, should be part of the study.

What Changes in Procedure

When the House resolves into Committee of the Whole, the Speaker leaves the Chair and a Committee Chair takes over. A few procedural rules change:

  • MPs can speak more than once to the same clause, unlike regular debate where each MP typically gets one turn
  • No quorum is required (unlike the regular House)
  • The process is somewhat more informal, allowing for back-and-forth questioning of ministers similar to a committee hearing

What You See in Hansard

Committee of the Whole sessions appear in Hansard as part of the regular sitting day transcript. They are identified by the Chair's designation and by the bill being studied. In epac, you will see these debates in the Parliament tab alongside regular debate; searching for a bill number will surface both the floor debate and Committee of the Whole proceedings.
