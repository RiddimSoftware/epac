What is a federal riding?

April 27, 2026

Canada is divided into 338 federal electoral districts, commonly called ridings or constituencies. Each riding elects one Member of Parliament (MP) to sit in the House of Commons in Ottawa.

Riding boundaries are not set by the government of the day. They are drawn by independent electoral boundaries commissions — one per province — established under the Electoral Boundaries Readjustment Act. The commissions use census population data to ensure each riding represents a roughly comparable number of residents.

Your riding is determined by your home address. Because postal codes do not map exactly to riding boundaries, the official method is to look up your address on the Elections Canada website at elections.ca. As a practical shortcut, entering your postal code in epac will identify your riding and your current MP.

The current 338 ridings were redrawn following the 2021 census redistribution and took effect for the 2025 federal election. The number of ridings has grown over time as Canada's population has increased — the House had 301 seats after the 2003 redistribution and 308 after 2013.

Source: Elections Canada — elections.ca
