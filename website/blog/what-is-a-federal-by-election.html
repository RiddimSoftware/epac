What is a Federal By-Election?

April 27, 2026 · 4 min read

When a federal riding becomes vacant between general elections, Canadians in that riding get to elect a new Member of Parliament through a by-election. Here is how they work, why they happen, and why political watchers pay close attention to them.

When Does a Seat Become Vacant?

A federal riding can become vacant for several reasons:

  • An MP dies in office
  • An MP resigns from Parliament (to run for a provincial election, accept a cabinet appointment requiring a Senate seat, or for personal reasons)
  • An MP is disqualified (conviction for a serious offence, for example)
  • An MP is appointed to the Senate or another position that bars concurrent membership in the Commons

Once a seat is declared vacant, the Speaker of the House notifies the Chief Electoral Officer of Canada, who then issues a writ of election. The government has 11 to 180 days to set the by-election date after the writ is issued.

How a By-Election Differs from a General Election

By-elections follow the same rules as general elections — same candidate eligibility, campaign finance limits, voter registration requirements — but they affect only one riding. Turnout is typically lower than in general elections because there is no national campaign narrative to drive voters to the polls.

By-elections are sometimes held in clusters: the government can time multiple writs to coincide, holding several by-elections on the same day. This reduces administrative costs and sometimes limits media focus on any single race.

Why By-Elections Matter

In a minority Parliament, every seat matters for the balance of power. A party that gains a seat in a by-election shifts the arithmetic, potentially strengthening or weakening the government's position. By-elections are also traditionally seen as mid-term opinion polls — voters sometimes use them to send a message to the governing party without triggering a full election.

For the winning candidate, a by-election result means joining Parliament mid-session. New MPs elected by by-election typically receive an orientation from the House administration before taking their seat.
