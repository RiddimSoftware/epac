How to Contact Your Federal MP

April 27, 2026 · 4 min read

Your Member of Parliament represents you in Ottawa. Writing to them is one of the most direct and effective civic actions you can take — more so than signing a petition, and far more personal than a social media post. Here is how to do it effectively.

Email: The Most Practical Choice

Email is the most common and practical way to reach an MP. Most MPs have a Hill Office email (firstname.lastname@parl.gc.ca) and a constituency office email. Emails go to staff who triage correspondence; a well-written, specific message will be flagged for the MP's attention and often generates a substantive reply.

What makes an effective email:

  • Identify yourself as a constituent. State your name and riding. MPs prioritize mail from their own constituents.
  • Be specific about the issue. Reference a particular bill number (e.g. C-50), a vote, or a policy. Vague "I support/oppose X" emails are noted but rarely generate detailed replies.
  • Ask a clear question or make a clear request. "Will you support Bill C-50 at third reading?" or "Please raise the issue of [topic] in committee."
  • Keep it to one page. Staff read hundreds of emails. A concise, focused message is more likely to get read fully.

Phone: Faster for Urgent Issues

For time-sensitive matters — a vote happening this week, an urgent constituency issue — calling the constituency office is often more effective than email. You will likely speak to a caseworker or assistant, not the MP directly. State your name, your issue, and what you want the MP to do.

Letter: Counts as Personal Correspondence

Physical mail to the House of Commons is postage-free when addressed to an MP on Parliament Hill. A handwritten or typed letter is still taken seriously, particularly for issues where the constituency wants to demonstrate engagement. It is slower than email but signals a higher level of commitment.

What to Expect in Reply

Most MPs reply to all constituent mail within two to four weeks. Replies range from a brief acknowledgment to a substantive letter from the MP or a junior minister. If an issue is time-sensitive (a vote is imminent), say so explicitly — a well-timed message before a recorded vote can have the most impact.
