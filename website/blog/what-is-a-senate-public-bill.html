What is a Senate Public Bill?

April 27, 2026 · 3 min read

Most federal legislation starts in the House of Commons. But bills can also originate in the Senate — and these Senate-originated bills are numbered differently and follow a slightly different path to becoming law.

The S- Prefix

Bills originating in the Senate are numbered with the prefix S- rather than C-. A government bill tabled first in the Senate is called a Senate Government Bill (e.g. S-4). A bill introduced by a senator on their own initiative is called a Private Senator's Bill — also numbered S- but from the range S-200 and up by convention.

Why Introduce Bills in the Senate?

Government bills are sometimes introduced first in the Senate for procedural efficiency — the Senate can begin its study while the House is occupied with other business, reducing the total calendar time needed to pass a bill. This is particularly common for non-controversial legislation, technical amendments, and bills requiring detailed expert study before House debate.

Private Senator's Bills, like Private Members' Bills in the House, allow senators to introduce legislation on issues they care about independently of the government's agenda. Because senators serve until 75 and are not subject to elections, they can advocate on long-term policy issues across multiple Parliaments.

The Path to Royal Assent

A Senate bill follows the same stages as a House bill — just in reverse order. It passes through Senate first and second readings, committee study, and third reading, then goes to the House for its own readings, committee study, and votes. Both chambers must pass identical text before the bill can receive Royal Assent.
