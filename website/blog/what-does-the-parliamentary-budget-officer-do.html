What Does the Parliamentary Budget Officer Do?

April 27, 2026 · 4 min read

The Parliamentary Budget Officer (PBO) is an independent officer who provides economic and financial analysis to Parliament and Canadians. The PBO's most important function is independently costing legislation — government bills, proposed programs, and opposition platforms — so that Parliament has a check on the government's own cost estimates.

Independent Cost Analysis

When the government introduces a major spending bill, it publishes its own estimate of what the program will cost. These government estimates are often optimistic. The PBO provides an independent second opinion using its own economic models and assumptions.

When the PBO's estimate differs substantially from the government's — as it frequently does on infrastructure projects, defence spending, and social programs — the difference becomes a political and policy issue. Which number is more credible? The PBO's independence from government gives its estimates significant weight in parliamentary debate.

How to Read a PBO Report

PBO reports are available at pbo-dpb.ca. Each report includes:

  • The costing scenario (what assumptions about uptake, eligibility, and economic conditions are used)
  • A central estimate and a range reflecting uncertainty
  • Comparison to the government's published estimate (if available)
  • Key risks and sensitivities

The gap between PBO and government estimates is the most important number. A PBO estimate significantly above the government's signals the program may cost more than budgeted; below, that the government is overstating cost to make the program seem difficult to reverse.

PBO Estimates in epac

When you open a bill in epac's Bill Tracker that has a PBO costing note, a PBO Cost Card appears in the bill detail view. This card shows the PBO's estimate, the government's estimate (if different), and a flag when the two diverge significantly. Tapping the card opens the full PBO report on pbo-dpb.ca.
