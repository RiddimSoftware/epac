What is Question Period?

April 27, 2026 · 4 min read

Every day the House of Commons sits, 45 minutes are set aside for MPs to put oral questions directly to the Prime Minister and cabinet ministers. This is Question Period — the most televised and politically charged part of the parliamentary day. Here is how it works.

When Does it Happen?

Question Period takes place Monday through Thursday from 2:15 to 3:00 PM, and on Fridays from 11:15 AM to 12:00 PM (when the House is sitting). It lasts exactly 45 minutes by the Standing Orders. The Speaker rotates between parties, giving each party a roughly proportional share of questions based on their seat count.

Oral Questions vs. Written Questions

Oral questions are asked without notice — the government does not know what questions are coming. Responses are limited to about 35 seconds, which often makes them more rhetorical than informative. Written questions (also called Questions on the Order Paper) allow for longer, more detailed answers and are submitted in advance; the government typically has 45 days to respond. Both types appear in Hansard.

What Makes a Good Question?

The Standing Orders require that questions be concise, relate to matters of urgent public importance, and be addressed to a minister responsible for the matter. In practice, the Speaker rarely rules questions out of order; Question Period is the opposition's primary daily opportunity to hold the government accountable on the record, and the drama of the exchange is a feature of the Westminster system.

Finding Question Period in epac
