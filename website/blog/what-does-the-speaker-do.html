What Does the Speaker of the House Do?

April 27, 2026 · 4 min read

If you read Hansard in epac, you will notice that most exchanges in the House are not directed at other MPs — they are directed at "Mr. Speaker" or "Madam Speaker." The Speaker of the House of Commons is the presiding officer of Parliament, and their role is more important to how the House functions than their low media profile might suggest.

How the Speaker is Chosen

The Speaker is elected by secret ballot of all MPs at the start of each Parliament. By convention, the Speaker comes from the governing party but is expected to act impartially once elected. The Speaker resigns from their party caucus upon election and, in a subsequent election, runs as "Speaker" rather than under a party banner.

What the Speaker Does

The Speaker's primary role is presiding over debates. From the Chair, the Speaker:

  • Recognizes MPs to speak — no MP can take the floor without being recognized by the Speaker
  • Enforces time limits on speeches and questions, as set by the Standing Orders
  • Rules on points of order — when an MP raises a procedural objection, the Speaker decides whether it is valid
  • Rules on questions of privilege — allegations that an MP's rights have been violated
  • Maintains decorum — warning or naming MPs who use unparliamentary language or disrupt proceedings

The Speaker's Vote

The Speaker does not vote in ordinary divisions. By convention rooted in the Westminster tradition, the Speaker only casts a vote to break a tie — and when doing so, votes to continue debate (rather than decide the issue), to give the House another chance to reach a majority decision.

What You See in Hansard

In Hansard transcripts in epac, the Speaker appears extensively. Their rulings on points of order and their comments maintaining order are verbatim in the record. When reading a debate, the Speaker's interventions are identified as "The Speaker" or their name, and their rulings often provide useful context for what was being argued procedurally at that moment in the debate.
