What Does the Ethics Commissioner Do?

April 27, 2026 · 3 min read

The Conflict of Interest and Ethics Commissioner is an officer of Parliament who administers two ethics codes: the Conflict of Interest Act for ministers and senior officials, and the Conflict of Interest Code for Members of the House of Commons. When an MP or minister is found to have violated these rules, the Commissioner's report becomes a major political story.

Two Separate Codes

The Commissioner administers ethics rules for two groups:

  • The Conflict of Interest Act applies to public office holders — ministers, parliamentary secretaries, and senior government officials. It sets rules around financial disclosures, recusal from decisions, and post-employment restrictions.
  • The Conflict of Interest Code for Members applies to all MPs, including backbenchers. It requires MPs to disclose outside income, gifts, and private interests that could conflict with their public duties.

How Investigations Work

The Commissioner can investigate on their own initiative or following a referral from a Member of Parliament. Investigations are conducted in private — the subject has the right to see the evidence and respond. Final reports are tabled in Parliament and immediately public.

If the Commissioner finds a violation, they issue a report with findings. Under the Conflict of Interest Act, they can also impose fines of up to $500 for public office holders. Findings of violations are not criminal — but they carry significant political weight. Several cabinet ministers have resigned following adverse findings.

The Commissioner and Parliament

The Commissioner is appointed by the Governor General on the recommendation of both chambers of Parliament, not the government alone. This independence is essential — the Commissioner must be able to investigate the very ministers who advised the appointment.

Reports are studied by the Standing Committee on Access to Information, Privacy and Ethics (ETHI), which can call the subject of a report to explain their conduct.
