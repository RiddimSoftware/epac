What Does the Privacy Commissioner Do?

April 27, 2026 · 3 min read

The Privacy Commissioner of Canada (OPC) is an independent officer of Parliament who oversees compliance with Canada's federal privacy laws and investigates privacy complaints from Canadians. Here is how the OPC works and why it matters.

Two Privacy Laws, One Commissioner

The OPC administers two federal laws:

  • The Privacy Act governs how federal government institutions collect, use, and disclose Canadians' personal information. If you believe a federal department mishandled your personal data, you can file a complaint with the OPC.
  • PIPEDA (the Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act) governs how private sector organizations collect and use personal information in the course of commercial activity — covering most Canadian businesses, particularly those operating across provincial borders.

Investigations and Complaints

The OPC investigates complaints from individuals who believe their privacy rights have been violated. Investigations can result in findings and recommendations — the OPC cannot issue binding orders under PIPEDA (though this may change under proposed reforms), but findings have significant reputational and regulatory consequences for organizations found to have violated the law.

The OPC also conducts systemic audits and studies — it has investigated Facebook, Google, Air Canada, and federal government departments. Reports are tabled in Parliament and attract considerable media attention.

Privacy and Parliament

The Privacy Commissioner reports to Parliament, not to government. This independence is crucial — the federal government is itself subject to the Privacy Act, and an OPC that reported to cabinet would face obvious conflicts. The Commissioner appears regularly before the Standing Committee on Access to Information, Privacy and Ethics (ETHI) to discuss annual reports and ongoing investigations.
