What Does the Commissioner of Official Languages Do?

April 27, 2026 · 3 min read

Canada has two official languages — English and French — and a federal law requiring their equal status. The Commissioner of Official Languages is the independent officer responsible for ensuring federal institutions comply with the Official Languages Act and for protecting the language rights of Canadians.

The Official Languages Act

The Official Languages Act (1969, updated 1988 and again in 2023) establishes the constitutional foundation for federal bilingualism. It requires federal institutions to:

  • Provide services in both official languages where there is significant demand
  • Work with employees in their preferred official language in designated bilingual regions
  • Promote the vitality of English and French minority communities

What the Commissioner Does

The Commissioner is an officer of Parliament who investigates complaints from Canadians who believe their language rights have been violated by a federal institution. The Commissioner can also conduct systemic audits of departments, recommend improvements, and report to Parliament.

The Commissioner has no binding order-making power — they can investigate and recommend, but cannot compel compliance. However, reports to Parliament and media attention create strong incentive for departments to address findings.

The Commissioner and Parliament

Each year the Commissioner tables an annual report in Parliament and appears before the Standing Committee on Official Languages. The bilingualism of Parliament itself — simultaneous interpretation, the right to speak French or English in the House — falls under the Commissioner's oversight.
