How to Read an MP's Party Solidarity Score

April 27, 2026 · 4 min read

Every MP profile in epac shows a party solidarity score — a percentage that measures how often an MP votes with the majority of their party caucus on recorded votes. Here is exactly what the number means, how it is computed, and what it tells you about an MP's independence or loyalty.

How It's Calculated

The score counts only decisive votes — those where the MP cast a Yea or Nay ballot (not Paired or Abstained). For each such vote, epac checks whether the MP's ballot matched the majority position of their party caucus on that division. The score is the percentage of decisive votes where the MP aligned with the party majority.

Absences (where the MP did not vote at all) are excluded — they have no recorded ballot, so they do not affect the score either way.

What the Score Signals

  • 95–100% — Votes almost entirely with the party line. This is typical for government backbenchers on confidence-related votes. It may reflect strong agreement, or it may reflect the constraints of caucus discipline.
  • 80–94% — Votes with the party most of the time, with occasional departures. These departures on specific bills or motions are often the most interesting data points to investigate.
  • Below 80% — Votes against the party majority frequently. This level of independence is rare and typically reflects an MP who has broken from caucus, is sitting as an independent, or represents a riding where the party's national position conflicts with local interests.

What It Doesn't Tell You

The score does not distinguish between free votes (where parties allow MPs to vote their conscience) and whipped votes (where the party expects all members to vote the same way). On free votes — social issues, Private Members' Bills — divergence is expected and encouraged. On budget votes, a government MP who votes against the party is making a historic statement; an opposition MP doing the same on their own party's motion is merely expressing disagreement.

Context matters. Look at which specific votes drove the score down, not just the percentage itself.

Finding it in epac

The party solidarity score appears on every MP's profile page, directly below their portrait. Tap the score to see a full breakdown: which votes matched the party majority and which did not, along with the option to view the full voting record.
