What Does the Auditor General Do?

April 27, 2026 · 5 min read

The Auditor General of Canada is an independent officer of Parliament whose job is to hold the federal government financially accountable. The OAG's reports — released several times per year — are some of the most significant accountability documents in Canadian governance. Here is how the AG works and why the reports matter.

Two Types of Audits

Financial audits verify that the government's financial statements accurately represent its finances. The AG audits the Public Accounts of Canada — the annual financial statements of the federal government — and issues an opinion on whether they present a "true and fair view." A "qualified" or "adverse" opinion signals a serious accounting problem.

Performance audits (also called value-for-money audits) examine whether government programs are achieving their objectives efficiently and effectively. These are more politically significant because they go beyond numbers — they can find that a program is poorly designed, wasteful, or not delivering intended benefits. Recent performance audits have examined Indigenous housing, pandemic spending, federal contracting, and government IT systems.

Reporting to Parliament

The AG reports to Parliament, not to cabinet. This independence is structural: the AG is appointed by the Governor General on the advice of the PM after consultation with opposition leaders, and serves a non-renewable 10-year term. The AG cannot be removed by the government without a parliamentary address.

Reports are tabled in Parliament and immediately studied by the Standing Committee on Public Accounts (PAC), which calls deputy ministers and senior officials to explain the findings. PAC hearings are some of the most pointed accountability exchanges in Parliament.

Notable Reports

Auditor General reports have exposed significant failures over the decades: the sponsorship scandal (2004), delays in fighter jet procurement, Indigenous services gaps, and pandemic-era benefit fraud. The AG has no power to compel action — only to report — but public attention to AG findings often creates political pressure for change.
