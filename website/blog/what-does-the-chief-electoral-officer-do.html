What Does the Chief Electoral Officer Do?

April 27, 2026 · 3 min read

Every federal election in Canada is administered by Elections Canada under the direction of the Chief Electoral Officer (CEO). The CEO is an officer of Parliament who operates independently of the government — a structural requirement to ensure the administration of democracy is not controlled by whoever currently holds power.

Running Federal Elections

The CEO's primary responsibility is administering elections under the Canada Elections Act. This includes:

  • Maintaining the National Register of Electors (the voters' list)
  • Hiring and training returning officers in all 338 ridings
  • Setting up and operating polling stations
  • Counting votes and declaring results
  • Administering candidate registration and party registration

Campaign Finance Oversight

Elections Canada also administers campaign finance rules: spending limits for candidates and parties, disclosure requirements, and the public funding system for political parties. The CEO can refer potential violations to the Commissioner of Canada Elections — a separate officer responsible for investigations and prosecution of electoral law offences.

Impartiality and Independence

The CEO cannot vote in federal elections — a symbolic but significant constraint that underlines the neutrality required of the role. The CEO is appointed by resolution of the House of Commons (not by the government alone) and can only be removed by Parliament through a joint address of both chambers. This double-lock on independence is essential: elections must be administered by someone no party can pressure or remove.
