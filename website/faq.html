Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about Canada's Parliament, your MP, and the epac app.
All answers are sourced from Parliament of Canada, Elections Canada, and other official authorities.

What is Hansard?

Hansard is the official verbatim transcript of debates in Canada's House of Commons and Senate. It records every word spoken in the chamber — speeches, questions, and responses. Published after each sitting day, Hansard is the authoritative public record of parliamentary debate. According to Parliament of Canada, Hansard has been published since 1880.

How do I find out who my MP is?

You can find your Member of Parliament by entering your postal code on Elections Canada's riding lookup or by using the epac app's My MP section. Canada has 343 federal electoral districts (ridings), each electing one MP to the House of Commons.

What is a federal riding?

A federal riding (also called an electoral district or constituency) is a geographic area that elects one Member of Parliament to the House of Commons. Canada has 343 federal ridings, determined by the Electoral Boundaries Redistribution Act. According to Elections Canada, boundaries are redrawn by independent provincial commissions after each census to reflect population changes.

What is Question Period?

Question Period is a 45-minute session held each sitting day where MPs — primarily from the opposition — can ask Cabinet ministers questions about government policy, spending, and actions. It is the primary public accountability mechanism in Parliament and is broadcast live on CPAC. According to Parliament of Canada, the tradition dates to 1867 and is based on the British parliamentary model.

How does a bill become law in Canada?

A bill must pass several stages in both the House of Commons and the Senate before becoming law: introduction (First Reading), debate on principles (Second Reading), committee study, report stage, and a final vote (Third Reading). The same process repeats in the other chamber. Once both chambers agree, the Governor General gives Royal Assent and the bill becomes an Act of Parliament. According to Parliament of Canada's LEGISinfo service, government bills have priority scheduling over private members' bills.

What is the difference between the House of Commons and the Senate?

Canada's Parliament has two chambers. The House of Commons has 343 elected MPs representing geographic ridings. The Senate has 105 senators appointed by the Governor General on the advice of the Prime Minister. All legislation must pass both chambers, though money bills must originate in the House. According to Parliament of Canada, the Senate's role is to provide independent "sober second thought" on legislation.

How do I contact my MP?

Every MP has two offices: one on Parliament Hill in Ottawa and one in their riding. Contact details — phone, mail, and email — are published on ourcommons.ca. The epac app displays your MP's contact information and lets you compose a pre-filled email from any bill, vote, or debate you are following.

What is prorogation?

Prorogation is the formal suspension of a parliamentary session by the Governor General, on the advice of the Prime Minister. When Parliament is prorogued, all bills that have not yet received Royal Assent die and must be reintroduced. MPs and senators keep their seats. According to Parliament of Canada, prorogation is a normal part of the parliamentary cycle that typically occurs when the government's legislative agenda for a session has been completed.

How often does Parliament sit?

The House of Commons typically sits from mid-September to late June, with breaks in fall, winter, and spring. In a typical year, Parliament sits approximately 130 days. The official sitting calendar is published by Parliament of Canada at ourcommons.ca. The epac app displays the sitting calendar and shows you the most recent Hansard debate.

What is a recorded vote?

A recorded vote (also called a division) is a vote in which each individual MP's choice is recorded in Hansard. Members vote by pressing a button on their desk. Recorded votes can be requested by any party whip or MP, and the full results — including how each member voted — are published in the official parliamentary record. According to Parliament of Canada, the results of every recorded division are published in the Journals of the House of Commons. The epac app shows all recorded votes and lets you see how your MP voted on any division.

What is a private member's bill?

A private member's bill is legislation introduced by any MP who is not a Cabinet minister. Unlike government bills, private members' bills receive limited debate time — they are drawn by lottery for spots in the schedule. According to Parliament of Canada, few private members' bills become law, but they are an important mechanism for raising issues and signalling policy priorities to constituents and to government.

What are MP expenses?

Under Parliament of Canada's Proactive Disclosure policy, MPs are required to publish their travel, hospitality, and contract expenses each quarter. These disclosures cover expenses incurred in the course of parliamentary duties and are published at ourcommons.ca/proactivedisclosure. The epac app shows MP expenditure disclosures so Canadians can see how public funds are being spent.

What is the Parliamentary Budget Officer?

The Parliamentary Budget Officer (PBO) is an independent officer of Parliament who provides objective analysis of federal finances and the cost of policy proposals. Created by the Federal Accountability Act (2006), the PBO can independently estimate the cost of any bill — including private members' bills — and reports to Parliament, not to the government. The epac app displays PBO cost estimates alongside bills when they are available.

What is the Auditor General of Canada?

The Auditor General of Canada is an independent officer of Parliament who audits federal departments and Crown corporations to ensure public funds are spent lawfully and as intended. The Auditor General reports findings directly to the House of Commons, not to the government. According to the Office of the Auditor General, their audits are the primary external check on federal government spending and management.
